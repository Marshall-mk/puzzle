What it is: X-rays use a small amount of ionizing radiation to create pictures of the inside of the body. Dense structures (like bone) show up white; softer tissues appear darker.

Best for:

  • Bones: fractures, dislocations, spine problems
  • Chest: lung infections (e.g., pneumonia), heart size
  • Teeth: dental evaluation

Why clinicians choose it: Fast, cheap, widely available, and great for bone & chest screening.

Age note: Children are more sensitive to radiation, so X-rays are used only when necessary and with minimized dose.

What it is: Ultrasound uses high-frequency sound waves, not radiation. A handheld probe sends sound into the body and measures echoes to form images.

Best for:

  • Pregnancy imaging
  • Abdomen: liver, gallbladder, kidneys
  • Pelvis: uterus, ovaries, prostate
  • Blood flow: using Doppler

Why clinicians choose it: Safe for all ages including babies and pregnant individuals, real-time imaging, great for soft tissues and fluid-filled organs.

Age note: Preferred for young patients because it avoids radiation.

What it is: A CT scanner uses rotating X-rays and computer processing to create 3D images of the body.

Best for:

  • Emergency cases: trauma, internal bleeding
  • Chest & abdomen: tumors, infections
  • Bones and spine
  • Lungs: pulmonary embolism, nodules

Why clinicians choose it: Very fast, clear 3D detail, and excellent for detecting emergencies.

Age note: Use cautiously in children due to higher radiation. Benefit must outweigh risk.

What it is: MRI uses strong magnets and radio waves—no radiation—to create very detailed images of soft tissues.

Best for:

  • Brain & spinal cord
  • Joints: knees, shoulders, ligaments, cartilage
  • Heart and vessels
  • Tumors & soft tissue diseases

Why clinicians choose it: Exceptional soft-tissue contrast, no radiation, and ideal for neurological and musculoskeletal imaging.

Age note: No radiation risk, but long scan times require staying still — younger children may need sedation.

What it is: PET uses a radioactive tracer to show how tissues use energy. Often paired with CT or MRI (e.g., PET-CT).

Best for:

  • Cancer detection and treatment monitoring
  • Brain metabolism: dementia, epilepsy
  • Heart perfusion

Why clinicians choose it: Shows function, not just structure; detects disease activity early; excellent for staging cancer.

Age note: Used mainly in adults unless clinically essential, due to radiation from tracer + CT.
